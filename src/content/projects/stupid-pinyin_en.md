Everyday Chinese input methods learn from the user, correct incomplete spellings, and predict whole words or phrases. Those conveniences become experimental confounds when a child is expected to identify a character rather than receive help from the keyboard.

Stupid Pinyin is a RIME-based scheme designed for controlled research input. It uses large 30 pt candidates, disables predictive completion and the user dictionary, and avoids fuzzy-correction rules. The result keeps Chinese typing simple and readable while giving literacy experiments tighter control over how an answer is produced.
